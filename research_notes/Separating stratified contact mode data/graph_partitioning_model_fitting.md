# Graph partitioning without a preset k, and model-based multi-structure fitting, for unsupervised contact-mode segmentation

Scope: (A) graph partitioning beyond normalized-cut spectral clustering, especially signed and boundary-driven methods that need no k; (B) model-based multi-structure fitting and learned primitive segmentation, extended beyond 3D point clouds. Everything is read against the user's problem. Peg-in-hole samples lie on a stratified contact surface in 4D reduced coordinates (rho, radial tilt, tangential tilt, depth). Each sample carries a pose, a measured wrench with friction, a local-PCA normal, curvature and trajectory links. There are about 1e6 samples, sampled very non-uniformly. Hook-in-eyelet uses 6-DoF free-space poses, with modes separated by narrow passages. Methods already tried: NJW spectral clustering with an eigengap k (ARI about 0.3 from pose only, about 0.85 with wrench), region growing, patch merging, HDBSCAN, GMM, effective-resistance cuts and ToMATo.

Convention: Cited Findings come from sources fetched or searched in this session, unless marked "(known reference, not re-fetched)". Application notes for the contact problem are my own reasoning and appear only under Inferences.

## Q1. Which k-free graph partitioning methods suit contact-mode discovery, and which keep thin, small clusters next to large ones? Covers correlation clustering, multicut and lifted multicut, mutex watershed, Leiden/Louvain/CPM, Markov stability, 1-spectral/TV/Potts, Felzenszwalb–Huttenlocher, and semi-supervised or active labelling.

### Takeaway
Signed-graph partitioning fits this problem best. That means minimum-cost multicut (correlation clustering), lifted multicut, mutex watershed and the GASP family. These methods take attractive and repulsive edge costs and get the number and sizes of clusters from the cost signs. They have no balance term, so a thin mode survives whenever its boundary edges are repulsive. The open problem moves from choosing k to calibrating edge costs, and the wrench and normal jumps the user already has are suited to that. Solvers reach the needed scale: RAMA on GPU handles about 1e8 edge variables in seconds, and GAEC/KLj, mutex watershed and parallel correlation clustering run on CPU. Balanced-cut objectives (Ncut, ratio and Cheeger cuts, 1-spectral, multiclass TV) and modularity, with its resolution limit, favour large clusters by construction. CPM-Leiden, LambdaCC and Markov stability give resolution sweeps rather than one k. If even a few labels are allowed, Poisson learning is the right semi-supervised tool.

### Cited Findings

#### Minimum-cost multicut / correlation clustering (MC) and lifted multicut (LMC)
- **Core idea and k:** For long-term point trajectories, the minimum-cost multicut formulation "can optimize both the cluster assignment and the number of clusters, and it allows varying cluster sizes, unlike the commonly used spectral clustering approach." It uses attractive and repulsive pairwise terms and outperformed spectral clustering on FBMS-59 and on the VSB100 motion subtask. — [Keuper, Andres, Brox, ICCV 2015](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2015/html/Keuper_Motion_Trajectory_Segmentation_ICCV_2015_paper.html)
- **Lifted multicut:** Lifted multicut adds long-range terms that change the objective but not the feasible set of the local graph. The authors propose two efficient primal-feasible heuristics, Greedy Additive Edge Contraction (GAEC) and Kernighan–Lin with joins (KLj). They give results near the state of the art on BSDS-500 pixel grids and state-of-the-art results on Princeton **mesh decomposition**, which segments a sampled surface into parts. — [Keuper et al., ICCV 2015 (arXiv 1505.06973)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.06973); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iccv/2015/keuper2015iccv-efficient)
- **Higher-order lifted multicut:** Costs over more than two nodes improved on the state of the art in motion segmentation (FBMS-59). — [Keuper, arXiv 1704.01811](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.01811)
- **RAMA (GPU multicut, CVPR 2022):** A primal-dual method that repeats three steps: find conflicted cycles, pass messages between edges and cycles, and contract edges using matrix multiplications. It is one to two orders of magnitude faster than sequential CPU solvers without meaningful quality loss, handles problems with up to about 1e8 variables in a few seconds, and gives lower bounds alongside primal solutions. — [Abbas & Swoboda, arXiv 2109.01838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.01838)
- **RAMA code:** `pip install git+https://github.com/pawelswoboda/RAMA.git`, then `rama_py.rama_cuda(i_list, j_list, cost_list, rama_py.multicut_solver_options("PD"))`. Modes are P (fastest), PD (default), PD+ (best quality) and D (lower bound only). **Positive cost means attractive (join); negative means repulsive.** It needs CUDA 11.2+. A CPU-only build exists for the C++ text-input tool only, not for the Python call. The repo is BSD-3 with 91 stars and 184 commits. — [RAMA GitHub](https://github.com/pawelswoboda/RAMA)
- **ClusterFuG (ICML 2023):** Multicut on the *complete* graph, with edge costs written as inner products of node feature vectors. This avoids building a graph and avoids quadratic cost. Adapted greedy algorithms handle tens of thousands of nodes. — [Abbas & Swoboda, PMLR 202](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/abbas23a.html)
- **Large-instance practice:** For connectomics-scale multicuts, a hierarchical block-wise (domain decomposition) solver showed no significant loss in quality compared with solving globally. — [Pape et al., ICCVW 2017](https://research.google/pubs/solving-large-multicut-problems-for-connectomics-via-domain-decomposition/)
- **Python tooling:** `python-elf` (`pip install python-elf` or `conda install -c conda-forge python-elf`) provides multicut, lifted multicut and other graph partitioning, implemented in `nifty`. The repo has 593 commits. It recently moved its compute-heavy code to bioimage-cpp, which "may lead to some bugs and incompatibilities." — [elf GitHub](https://github.com/constantinpape/elf)
- **LambdaCC:** Correlation clustering with one resolution parameter λ that "interpolates between the sparsest cut objective and the cluster deletion problem." The framework generalizes modularity, sparsest cut, normalized cut and cluster deletion. — [Veldt, Gleich, Wirth, arXiv 1712.05825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.05825); [Veldt et al. 2019 (resolution parameters)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1903.05246)
- **Parallel correlation clustering:** A shared-memory parallel framework on the LambdaCC objective, run on graphs with billions of edges, gave up to 28.44x speed-up over the authors' sequential version. Code is at `jeshi96/parallel-correlation-clustering`. — [Shi, Dhulipala et al., PVLDB 14(11) 2021](https://mail.vldb.org/pvldb/volumes/14/paper/Scalable%20Community%20Detection%20via%20Parallel%20Correlation%20Clustering); [arXiv 2108.01731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01731v1)

#### Mutex watershed (MWS) and GASP
- **MWS:** A greedy algorithm for signed graph partitioning with attractive and repulsive cues. It needs no seeds or threshold, and the number of segments is not fixed. The authors prove it solves, to global optimality, an objective closely tied to the multicut/correlation-clustering ILP. It is deterministic, and its runtime is empirically near-linearithmic. Short-range attractive plus long-range repulsive CNN cues gave the best published ISBI 2012 result at the time. — [Wolf et al., TPAMI 43(10) 2021, arXiv 1904.12654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12654); [ECCV 2018 version](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2018/papers_ECCV/papers/Steffen_Wolf_The_Mutex_Watershed_ECCV_2018_paper.pdf)
- **GASP (CVPR 2022):** Generalized hierarchical agglomerative clustering on signed graphs. The algorithms differ only in the linkage criterion and in whether cannot-link constraints are used. GAEC, greedy fixation and MWS are special cases, and new average, max and min linkage variants are added. Some variants define an ultrametric. Without superpixels it gave state-of-the-art results on CREMI 2016. — [Bailoni et al., CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Bailoni_GASP_a_Generalized_Framework_for_Agglomerative_Clustering_of_Signed_Graphs_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf); [arXiv 1906.11713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.11713)
- **GASP code:** `GASP.segmentation.run_GASP(graph, signed_edge_weights, linkage_criteria=...)`, where the graph is a `nifty.graph.UndirectedGraph` and **positive weights are attractive, negative repulsive**. Linkage options are mean/average, max/single, min/complete, mutex_watershed/abs_max, sum, quantile, generalized_mean and smooth_max. Cannot-link constraints and edge sizes are optional. Install is conda-only (`conda create -n GASP -c conda-forge -c abailoni gasp`). The repo is small (15 stars), and its maintenance status is unclear. — [GASP GitHub](https://github.com/abailoni/GASP)

#### Modularity / CPM / Leiden / Markov stability / Infomap
- **Leiden:** Louvain can produce badly connected or even disconnected communities: up to 25% badly connected and 16% disconnected in experiments. Leiden adds a refinement phase and guarantees connected communities. It optimizes modularity or the **Constant Potts Model (CPM)**, which is resolution-limit-free. In CPM, γ acts as a density threshold: communities should have internal density of at least γ, and the density between communities should be below γ. — [Traag, Waltman, van Eck, Sci. Rep. 2019 (arXiv 1810.08473)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08473); [igraph cluster_leiden docs](https://r.igraph.org/reference/cluster_leiden.html)
- **Signed graphs in leidenalg:** "Most methods (except CPM) only accept positive weights." The documented workaround splits the graph into a positive layer and a negative layer and optimizes them jointly with `layer_weights=[1,-1]`. — [leidenalg multiplex docs](https://leidenalg.readthedocs.io/en/latest/multiplex.html)
- **Fast parallel Leiden:** A shared-memory implementation exists. — [GVE-Leiden, arXiv 2312.13936](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.13936v6)
- **Markov stability:** A partition's quality is the clustered autocovariance of a Markov process on the graph, and Markov time acts as an intrinsic resolution. Modularity and normalized cut are its one-step special cases, and Fiedler spectral bisection emerges at long times. Partitions that stay optimal over long time spans are the robust ones. — [Delvenne, Yaliraki, Barahona, PNAS 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919907); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0812.1811)
- **PyGenStability:** A Python package for multiscale community detection. It maximizes generalized Markov stability with Louvain or Leiden, supports weighted undirected, directed and **signed** graphs and user-defined quality functions, and automatically detects robust partitions across scales. GPL; published as ACM TOMS Algorithm 1044 (2024). — [Arnaudon et al., arXiv 2303.05385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05385); [Imperial repository](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/0088382a-1d6b-424d-93fc-e489c5bd942b)
- **Infomap / map equation:** Scores a partition by the description length of a random walk. The best partition is the one in which flow stays trapped in modules ("modularity asks how the network is wired, the map equation how it is used"). It is multi-level, supports state (memory) networks, installs with `pip install infomap` and runs as `infomap.find_communities(G)`. — [Infomap Python docs](https://mapequation.org/infomap-python-docs/); [map equation concept](https://mapequation.org/infomap-python-docs/concepts/the-map-equation.html)

#### Balanced cuts: 1-spectral, p-Laplacian, total variation
- **1-spectral clustering:** The second eigenvalue of the nonlinear graph 1-Laplacian equals the optimal Cheeger cut. A nonlinear inverse power method converges to a nonlinear eigenvector and improves on p-spectral clustering in both Cheeger-cut quality and runtime. — [Hein & Bühler, NIPS 2010 (arXiv 1012.0774)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.0774). Code (Matlab/C) is on the [Saarland IPM page](https://www.ml.uni-saarland.de/code/IPM/IPM.htm).
- **Multiclass TV clustering:** Recursive TV bisection gives poor results for many clusters, so the authors use a non-recursive multiclass TV framework. It beats earlier TV algorithms and is competitive with NMF. — [Bresson, Laurent, Uminsky, von Brecht, NeurIPS 2013 (arXiv 1306.1185)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.1185)

#### Graph Potts / Mumford–Shah: cut pursuit
- **Cut pursuit:** A working-set method for graph-TV-regularized problems that recursively splits the connected components of the current solution using graph cuts. It covers convex TV and the nonconvex ℓ0 (Mumford–Shah/Potts) case, where the regularizer is "the weight of the cut between constant components" and so suits segmentation. The **number of pieces is an output**. It is fast when the solution has few level sets. C/C++ with Python and Matlab interfaces. — [Landrieu & Obozinski, AISTATS 2016 / SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v51/landrieu16.html); [cut-pursuit GitHub](https://github.com/loicland/cut-pursuit)
- **Extension:** Covers objectives with separable non-differentiable terms and includes a heuristic for multidimensional vertex values. — [Raguet & Landrieu, arXiv 1802.04383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.04383)

#### Felzenszwalb–Huttenlocher (FH)
- **Method:** FH merges two components across an edge, processed in increasing weight order, when the edge weight is below MInt(C1,C2) = min(Int(C1) + k/|C1|, Int(C2) + k/|C2|). Here Int(C) is the largest MST edge inside C, so the k/|C| term favours **larger** components. It runs in nearly linear time in the number of edges. — [Charles Reid wiki summary](https://charlesreid1.com/wiki/Graphs/Minimum_Spanning_Tree/Image_Segmentation); [UNC report](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~sahil/data/Segmentation-Report.pdf) (secondary sources; the IJCV 2004 primary text was not retrieved)

#### Local (seeded) clustering
- **LocalGraphClustering:** Finds a cluster around seed nodes without processing the whole graph and improves existing clusters with flow-based methods. Python over C++, `pip install localgraphclustering`, alpha status. — [PyPI](https://pypi.python.org/pypi/localgraphclustering/0.4.1); [Fountoulakis et al., arXiv 2004.09608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.09608)

#### Semi-supervised and active labelling with a few labels
- **Poisson learning:** Laplace (harmonic) learning degenerates at very low label rates. Poisson learning instead places sources and sinks at the labelled points and solves a graph Poisson equation, which is provably more stable. Poisson-MBO can include class-size priors. It beat recent methods at low label rates on MNIST, FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10. — [Calder et al., ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/calder20a.html)
- **graphlearning package:** `pip install graphlearning`, with optional `annoy` for approximate kNN and `torch` for GPU. It covers semi-supervised learning, **active learning** and clustering. The repo has 372 commits and CI. — [GraphLearning GitHub](https://github.com/jwcalder/GraphLearning)

**Why the user's spectral runs behave as observed.** Ncut, NJW, Cheeger and TV all minimize cut weight divided by a volume or size term, so a thin, low-volume stratum is never a cheap cluster. That explains "large clusters swallow thin modes" and the "balanced-cut bias." Eigengap k is unreliable because a stratified surface with tangent-continuous boundaries gives no clean spectral gap. Multicut-type objectives drop the denominator. The cluster count and sizes follow from where the edge costs turn negative, so modelling effort goes into calibrating edge costs (Q2) instead of choosing k.

**Recipe for applying this to peg-in-hole (A-pipeline).**
1. Build the graph as now: a symmetric kNN in the existing pose metric (d² = dρ² + d_depth² + L²(dθr² + dθt²)), k about 10 to 20, plus trajectory links as extra edges. With 1e6 nodes and k = 15 this gives about 1.5e7 edges, well inside RAMA's demonstrated range.
2. Compute per-edge jump statistics (Q2): angle between force directions; change in torque about the tip; Frobenius distance between the sign-free normal outer products; change in surface variation (local dimension); and change in principal curvatures. Curvature matters because it is the only geometric cue at *tangent-continuous* boundaries, where the normal does not jump.
3. Map the statistics to a boundary probability p_ij and set the cost c_ij = log((1−p_ij)/p_ij) + log((1−β)/β). This logit cost is standard multicut practice, but I did not verify a primary source for it in this session. β is the single prior knob; sweeping it gives a hierarchy rather than a k.
4. Add lifted edges. Make them **repulsive** between pose-near but non-adjacent samples (inside radius r, outside the kNN set) whose wrench directions differ strongly. Along a trajectory, make them **attractive** between samples linked by a walk segment with no wrench jump. Solve with lifted multicut (elf/nifty GAEC then KLj). If only local edges are kept, RAMA works on GPU.
5. A cheap first experiment: run GASP average linkage or MWS on the same signed weights for a deterministic baseline, then RAMA multicut for a better objective, and compare ARI against the analytic "mode" truth already in `mode_discovery_bench.py`.

**Thin two-contact strata.** Two-contact modes are intersections of sheets: 2D sets in 4D, codimension 2. A multicut separates them only if edges inside the intersection are attractive and edges from the intersection to each adjacent sheet are repulsive. With pose and normal cues alone, an intersection point looks like one side of a crease. The wrench is what makes intersection samples look different from both sheets, since two contact normals combine inside the friction cones. Add a per-node "local dimension" or "wrench not explained by one normal" feature, so that edges between sheet samples and intersection samples get repulsive costs.

**Hook-in-eyelet.** Modes are free-space regions joined through narrow passages. That is a flow-bottleneck structure, which suits Markov stability, Infomap or PCCA+ (Q5) on the sampler's transition or trajectory graph better than signed cuts. Edge costs there would only restate low conductance.

**If labels are allowed.** One or two labelled samples per mode would turn this into Poisson learning on the same wrench-weighted graph, plus active learning that asks for labels where the label field is ambiguous. That is likely the cheapest way to beat 0.85 ARI if the user accepts a few labels; I have not tested this.

- I did not verify PyGenStability runtime at 1e5–1e6 nodes. Markov stability needs a diffusion operator at each Markov time, and whether the package avoids dense matrix exponentials at that scale is unconfirmed.
- I did not confirm whether the Hein–Bühler 1-spectral code has a maintained Python port; only Matlab/C was found.
- The FH internal-difference formula comes from secondary sources; I did not retrieve the IJCV 2004 primary text.

- I did not retrieve a standard primary citation for the logit cost transform log((1−p)/p) used in multicut pipelines.

## Q2. How can edge or boundary probabilities be estimated on kNN graphs from local feature jumps, and how should one over-segment and then agglomerate (watershed or superpixels, then GASP)?

### Takeaway
Two patterns are well established. First, take edge affinities or boundary probabilities, from a learned model or from statistics, and feed them to a signed partitioner (MWS, GASP, multicut); this is the standard connectomics pipeline. Second, build a hierarchical watershed on an edge-weighted graph (Higra), cut it, then agglomerate with signed linkage. On a kNN graph of samples, boundary probability can be estimated from (i) jumps in the user's features (wrench direction, normal, curvature, local dimension), (ii) graph-based two-sample tests between the two endpoint neighbourhoods (Friedman–Rafsky / Chen–Friedman edge-count statistics), (iii) boundary-distance estimators for point clouds (Calder–Park–Slepčev), and (iv) local-PCA affinities designed for intersecting manifolds (Arias-Castro–Lerman–Zhang). Fused-penalty models (network lasso, cut pursuit) combine estimation and segmentation.

### Cited Findings
- **Graph two-sample tests:** The Friedman–Rafsky edge-count test counts cross-sample edges in a similarity graph such as the MST over the pooled samples. Chen and Friedman's generalized edge-count statistic also detects scale alternatives, which the original misses in moderate or high dimensions. Denser graphs (for example a 5-MST) work better than the plain MST, and a weighted variant fixes power loss when sample sizes differ. All of these are in the R package `gTests`, with asymptotic and permutation p-values. — [Chen & Friedman, arXiv 1307.6294](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1307.6294); [gTests manual](https://cran.r-universe.dev/gTests/doc/manual.html); [Zhang & Chen, arXiv 1711.04349](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.04349)
- **Boundary estimation from point clouds:** Computable estimators for the normal to the boundary, the distance to the boundary, and a test for whether a point lies in a boundary strip. They come with rigorous error estimates and are more accurate than earlier ones; detected boundary points are then used to impose PDE boundary conditions on point clouds. — [Calder, Park, Slepčev, J. Sci. Comput. 2022 (arXiv 2111.03217)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03217)
- **Spectral clustering on local PCA:** Runs local PCA in selected neighbourhoods, builds a kNN graph weighted by agreement of point and local-subspace (tangent) information, then applies spectral clustering. It is designed for **intersecting** clusters, which pairwise-distance spectral methods fail on, and comes with theory for simplified variants. — [Arias-Castro, Lerman, Zhang, JMLR 18(9) 2017](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/14-318.html)
- **Network lasso:** Generalizes the group lasso to graphs. Each node gets its own model parameters, and an edge penalty on parameter differences produces clustering and fitting together. It is solved by distributed ADMM with guaranteed convergence on large graphs, and a non-convex extension is included. — [Hallac, Leskovec, Boyd, KDD 2015 (arXiv 1507.00280)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.00280)
- **Hierarchical watershed (Higra):** Higra is a C++/Python sparse-graph library (`pip install higra`, binaries for Python 3.8–3.12 on amd64). `watershed_hierarchy_by_area`, `..._by_dynamics` and related functions build hierarchical watersheds on an arbitrary edge-weighted graph, following Cousty and Najman. Results are trees with altitudes that can be cut at any level. — [Higra docs](https://higra.readthedocs.io/en/latest/python/watershed_hierarchy.html); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/higra/0.6.7)
- **Affinities into signed agglomeration:** In GASP and MWS, CNN-predicted short-range attractive and long-range repulsive affinities feed a signed agglomeration. GASP is state of the art on CREMI **without** superpixels, and MWS was best on ISBI 2012 at the time. — [Bailoni et al., CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Bailoni_GASP_a_Generalized_Framework_for_Agglomerative_Clustering_of_Signed_Graphs_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf); [Wolf et al., arXiv 1904.12654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12654)
- **Edge detection helps primitive segmentation:** SED-Net detects surface patches and edges jointly and reports that each helps the other. HPNet feeds an adjacency matrix encoding **sharp edges**, plus spectral descriptors from predicted geometric parameters, into mean-shift clustering. — [SED-Net, SIGGRAPH 2023 (GitHub)](https://github.com/yuanqili78/SED-Net); [HPNet, ICCV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Yan_HPNet_Deep_Primitive_Segmentation_Using_Hybrid_Representations_ICCV_2021_paper.html)
- **Fused piecewise-constant segmentation:** Cut pursuit (ℓ0/Potts) segments a graph signal into constant pieces, with the number of pieces as an output, and handles multidimensional values heuristically. — [Landrieu & Obozinski](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v51/landrieu16.html); [Raguet & Landrieu, arXiv 1802.04383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.04383)

### Inferences
- **Per-edge boundary statistic for the contact data.** For an edge (i,j), let A be N(i)\N(j) and B be N(j)\N(i), using each endpoint's kNN set in pose space. Test whether the *wrench directions* (or force directions, or sign-free normals) in A and B come from the same distribution. Use the generalized edge-count statistic on a small kNN graph over A∪B in feature space, or an energy or MMD statistic. Its z-score or p-value is a boundary probability that adapts to local noise and needs no global threshold. This targets the user's two failure modes. It is density-robust, because it compares distributions rather than raw distances, which matters with very non-uniform sampling. It also detects tangent-continuous boundaries when the wrench jumps there, for example when the contact point moves to a different feature so the torque changes even though the C-space normal is continuous.
- **Calibrating p without labels.** On most edges the statistic is null, because most edges are interior. Fit a two-component mixture (null versus boundary) to the edge statistics and read p_ij from the posterior, or apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR to the p-values. Either gives costs for Q1's multicut with no k.
- **Sim-supervised calibration.** The simulator gives contact pairs for free. Train a small edge classifier (logistic regression or gradient boosting on the jump features) on a *different* peg/hole geometry, or on the analytic model, then apply it to new data. This is the connectomics pattern: learned affinities followed by MWS, GASP or multicut. Using the target geometry's own contact labels would leak privileged information, which the benchmark currently reserves for scoring.
- **Fused per-node models (network lasso / cut pursuit).** Give each sample a local affine model: its tangent plane, or a linear map from pose to wrench direction. Penalize differences across edges with an ℓ2 group penalty (network lasso) or ℓ0 (cut pursuit). Neighbourhoods inside one mode share a model, while across a crease or a contact switch the models differ, so the fused-model clusters are mode candidates. This is the graph counterpart of PEARL (Q3) with *local* rather than global models.
- **Over-segment then agglomerate.** (1) Compute a node "boundariness" (max or mean of incident p_ij), or use p_ij directly as edge altitude. (2) Build a Higra watershed hierarchy *by dynamics* (contrast) rather than *by area*, so thin regions with high-contrast boundaries are not pruned for being small; this follows from what dynamics measures and is untested here. (3) Cut to an over-segmentation of perhaps 1e3–1e4 regions. (4) Build a region adjacency graph with signed costs from aggregated edge evidence, plus model-fit tests (Q3: does one constraint model explain both regions?). (5) Agglomerate with GASP average linkage or multicut. This is the user's "patch over-segmentation + merge" with two changes: merge decisions become signed and k-free, and they can use model-fit evidence.
- **Local-PCA affinities (Arias-Castro et al.).** These are the right *graph-construction* fix for intersecting sheets. Weight edges by agreement of tangent subspaces, not only distance, so points of two sheets near their intersection are not strongly linked. The user's "normal" feature already does this; the paper supplies theory and recommended neighbourhood selection.

### Gaps
- I found no work applying graph two-sample tests per edge as a boundary detector on kNN graphs of manifold samples. The construction above is mine.
- Calder–Park–Slepčev estimate the boundary of the sampled domain, such as the edge of a sheet. Whether their estimators detect internal creases or stratum intersections, which are not boundaries of the support, is unverified.
- I did not find any learned edge-detector for point clouds of dimension above 3. PIE-Net, PCEDNet and EC-Net are 3D-specific and were not fetched.

## Q3. Can multi-model fitting (sequential/multi-RANSAC, J-/T-Linkage, MCT/MultiLink, PEARL, Multi-X, Progressive-X/X+, CONSAC/PARSAC, Efficient RANSAC, GlobFit) work when each contact pair gives a parametric constraint family in 4–6D?

### Takeaway
Yes. Multi-model fitting suits the peg-in-hole case better than generic clustering, because each single-contact mode is, by construction, the zero set of one analytic constraint family with a few unknown parameters. The two families of methods fit together. Preference and linkage methods (J-/T-Linkage, MCT, MultiLink) determine the number of models via outlier and model-selection rules. Energy methods (PEARL, Multi-X, Progressive-X) determine it via label costs, with spatial smoothness on the kNN graph. MCT and MultiLink handle *nested heterogeneous* classes, such as a sheet (one constraint) versus an intersection (two constraints), which matches the stratified structure. The obstacle is engineering, not concept. Most maintained code is specialized to 2-view geometry (homographies, fundamental matrices, 6D poses) or 3D primitives (Efficient RANSAC in CGAL), so the user must supply a custom model class (minimal solver plus residual) in 4D or 6D.

### Cited Findings
- **Efficient RANSAC:** Detects planes, spheres, cylinders, cones and tori in unorganized point clouds, decomposing point sets of several million samples in under a minute. CGAL's Shape Detection package implements it and lets users add custom shape types. — [Schnabel, Wahl, Klein, CGF 26(2) 2007](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v26i2pp214-226); [PDF](https://cg.cs.uni-bonn.de/backend/v1/files/publications/schnabel-2007-efficient.pdf); [CGAL Shape Detection](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Shape_detection/)
- **GlobFit:** Starts from RANSAC-fitted local primitives, then progressively learns relations between them (orientation, placement, equality) and enforces them in a global alignment step. Research-only code is on Google Code. — [Li et al., ACM TOG 30(4) 2011](https://geometry.stanford.edu/lgl_2024/papers/lwcscm-gfcfpdgr-11/lwcscm-gfcfpdgr-11.pdf); [code archive](https://code.google.com/archive/p/globfit)
- **J-Linkage → T-Linkage:** T-Linkage replaces J-Linkage's binary preference sets (each point's consensus over random hypotheses, clustered by Jaccard distance) with continuous preferences and Tanimoto distance. The journal version adds outlier rejection and **inlier-threshold estimation**. The number of models comes from agglomeration stopping plus outlier rejection, not a preset k. — [Magri & Fusiello, CVPR 2014](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2014/html/Magri_T-Linkage_A_Continuous_2014_CVPR_paper.html); [journal version](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1120867/1/jvci-t-linkage.pdf)
- **MCT (CVPR 2019):** Multi-class cascaded T-Linkage handles *nested classes* of models: lines and conics, homographies and fundamental matrices, planes and cylinders. — [Magri & Fusiello, multi-class T-Linkage PDF](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/1188418/1/paper%20multi%20class%20tlinkage.pdf)
- **MultiLink (CVPR 2021):** Fits models on the fly and runs model selection *inside the linkage rule* that decides whether two clusters merge, across multiple model classes. It is faster than preference-analysis methods, less sensitive to the inlier threshold, and compensates for weak hypothesis sampling. Points are embedded as preference vectors and compared with Tanimoto distance. Code is at github.com/magrilu/multilink. — [Magri, Leveni, Boracchi, CVPR 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/html/Magri_MultiLink_Multi-Class_Structure_Recovery_via_Agglomerative_Clustering_and_Model_Selection_CVPR_2021_paper.html); [poster](https://boracchi.faculty.polimi.it/docs/2021_CVPR_MultiLink_poster.pdf); [arXiv 2505.10874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10874)
- **PEARL:** Treats multi-model fitting as labelling with a global energy: geometric error, plus spatial regularity of inlier clusters, plus **label costs**. RANSAC-style proposals alternate with α-expansion and re-estimation. It "automatically select[s] a small number of models that best explain the whole data set." — [Isack & Boykov, IJCV 97(2) 2012](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~yboykov/Papers/ijcv10_pearl.pdf); [abstract](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~yboykov/Abstracts/ijcv10_pearl-abs.shtml)
- **Multi-X (ECCV 2018):** Multi-class, multi-instance fitting. It extends α-expansion with a label move that replaces a group of labels by a density mode in parameter space (mode-seeking with automatic bandwidth), and uses cross-validation to drop statistically insignificant instances. It was demonstrated on simultaneous plane+cylinder and line+circle fitting. — [Barath & Matas, arXiv 1706.00827](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1706.00827)
- **Progressive-X (ICCV 2019):** Interleaves hypothesis sampling with consolidation of the current interpretation: new hypotheses are proposed, cheaply rejected, or integrated through labelling-energy minimization. It is *anytime*. — [Barath & Matas, ICCV 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Barath_Progressive-X_Efficient_Anytime_Multi-Model_Fitting_Algorithm_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- **Progressive-X+ / clustering in consensus space (CVPR 2023):** Finds an unknown number of models by clustering in the consensus space. It uses a sampler that returns connected components of a *progressively densified neighbourhood graph* and is at least two orders of magnitude faster than competitors on two-view motion. Code: `danini/clustering-in-consensus-space`. — [Barath et al., arXiv 2103.13875](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.13875); [CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Barath_Finding_Geometric_Models_by_Clustering_in_the_Consensus_Space_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- **Progressive-X code:** C++ with pybind11 bindings (`src/pyprogressivex`), built from conda. Examples cover homography, fundamental matrix, 6D pose and vanishing points. Dependencies are Eigen, OpenCV, GFlags and GLog; license MIT; 80 stars. Custom model types are not documented on the page. — [progressive-x GitHub](https://github.com/danini/progressive-x)
- **CONSAC (CVPR 2020):** A network conditioned on previously found models steers RANSAC sampling to recover instances one at a time. It can be trained **self-supervised**; PyTorch code is at `fkluger/consac`. — [Kluger et al., CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Kluger_CONSAC_Robust_Multi-Model_Fitting_by_Conditional_Sample_Consensus_CVPR_2020_paper.html)
- **PARSAC (AAAI 2024):** Predicts several sets of sample and inlier weights *in parallel*, one per candidate instance, then fits each RANSAC-style. It is trained with task losses and **no ground-truth segmentation**, runs about two orders of magnitude faster than CONSAC, and takes as little as 5 ms. — [Kluger & Rosenhahn, arXiv 2401.14919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14919)
- **Algebraic (GPCA-type) view:** A union of subspaces is the zero set of polynomials. Gradients of the vanishing polynomials at a data point give normals to the piece through that point, and filtrated algebraic subspace clustering handles pieces of unequal dimension. The estimates are sensitive to noise. — [Tsakiris & Vidal, arXiv 1510.04396](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.04396)

### Inferences
**Model families for peg-in-hole (B-pipeline).** The repo's `peg_hole_analytic.py` states that every contact involves one of four circles. They are the peg tip or shoulder edge against the hole, or the hole's inner or outer rim against the peg. The separation is the minimum of four 1D circle searches, and each zero local minimum is one contact point labelled by a peg feature × hole feature pair. Three model families follow, from most to least prior knowledge:
1. **Known-type, unknown-parameter family (recommended).** Model M_(a,b)(θ) is the zero set {q : d_ab(q; θ) = 0}, where d_ab is the signed distance between peg feature a and hole feature b. Peg feature a is a circle of radius r_p at axial offset h_p. Hole feature b is a circle of radius r_h at height z_h, or a plane, or a cylinder wall. θ has about 2–4 parameters, so minimal samples are 2–4 points; RANSAC needs few iterations and hypotheses are cheap to verify. Single-contact modes are single models. Two-contact modes are points with low residual to two models, a nested "intersection" class in MCT/MultiLink terms. *Number of models* comes from label costs (PEARL/Progressive-X) or MultiLink's model selection.
2. **Generic implicit polynomial in 4D.** Degree d has C(4+d,d) monomials: 15 for d=2, 35 for d=3, 70 for d=4. Minimal sets are one fewer than that, which is impractical for global random sampling with many structures. It becomes workable if minimal sets are drawn inside kNN balls, as in Efficient RANSAC and Progressive-X+'s neighbourhood-graph sampler, or if the user's existing local quadratic fits serve as hypotheses (one per seed point).
3. **Local-chart hypotheses for T-Linkage or MultiLink.** Each seed sample's local quadric fit is a hypothesis, and each point's preference vector holds its residuals to nearby hypotheses (sparse, via a kNN radius). Tanimoto-linkage agglomeration merges seeds whose hypotheses explain the same points. This is close to the user's patch-merge approach but with a principled, model-based merge criterion and outlier and threshold estimation.

**Use the wrench as an oriented constraint.** Efficient RANSAC accepts inliers only if their normal agrees with the model normal. I recall this from the paper, but it was not re-verified in the fetched text. Analogously, add a residual on the angle between the measured contact force, pulled back to C-space, and ∇_q d_ab(q; θ). Without friction the force is parallel to the constraint gradient; with friction it lies in a cone around it, so use a cone-violation residual. This doubles the information per sample, and wrench features are what raised ARI from 0.3 to 0.85.

**Energy.** E(L, Θ) = Σ_i residual(q_i, w_i; θ_{L_i}) + λ Σ_{(i,j)∈kNN ∪ traj} [L_i ≠ L_j]·w_ij + Σ_models h_model. The terms are data, Potts smoothness on the kNN and trajectory graph, and label cost (MDL). This is PEARL / Progressive-X. The label cost h and the inlier threshold set the number of modes, and T-Linkage's journal version estimates the threshold automatically. Intersection strata can be given extra composite labels (a, b)∧(c, d), as in MCT and Multi-X heterogeneous classes, with the residual taken as the max of the two.

**GlobFit-style relations.** After fitting, enforce shared parameters, for example the same tip-edge radius across all modes involving the peg tip edge. This recovers a consistent part geometry and could reveal the four circles from data alone, a by-product useful for identifying the part.

**Which code to start from.** None of the maintained packages exposes arbitrary-dimensional custom models in Python out of the box. The cheapest route is a short custom implementation: local minimal-set RANSAC proposals with a custom residual, then a labelling step. The labelling step can be α-expansion with label costs through a gco wrapper (not verified this session), or the Q1 multicut with costs from model-residual agreement. MultiLink (MATLAB/Python status not verified) and Progressive-X (C++, so a model class must be added) are second choices.

**Hook-in-eyelet.** Modes are free-space regions without a constraint equation, so model fitting does not apply directly. The only analogue is fitting the narrow-passage *boundaries* (contact constraint sheets) that separate the regions, which belongs to the C-obstacle problem rather than to segmenting free-space samples.

### Gaps
- I found no paper applying RANSAC-family multi-model fitting to robot configuration-space contact manifolds. The closest are 3D primitive fitting and 2-view geometry.
- I could not verify whether MultiLink's released code is MATLAB or Python, or whether Progressive-X accepts user-defined model classes from Python.
- I did not verify that a maintained Python gco/α-expansion wrapper with label costs exists.
- PEARL's scaling to 1e6 points with thousands of proposals was not checked. α-expansion cost grows with labels × points × edges, so subsampling or over-segmentation into superpoints first is probably needed (my inference).

## Q4. What transfers from learned primitive and CAD segmentation (SPFN, ParSeNet, HPNet, SED-Net, BPNet, Point2CAD, ComplexGen) and from sharp-feature implicit surfaces (NH-Rep, Neural-Singular-Hessian, SharpNet) to higher-dimensional configuration-space samples, for example via simulator self-supervision?

### Takeaway
Three ideas transfer. (1) **Min/Boolean-of-K implicit functions (NH-Rep style).** The peg–hole separation is a minimum over per-feature-pair distances, so a network f(q) = min_k f_k(q), fitted to signed-separation queries from the simulator, recovers modes as argmin pieces with no mode labels. This is self-supervised from geometry. (2) **Per-point embedding plus differentiable model fitting plus mean-shift (SPFN, ParSeNet, HPNet).** The number of segments is k-free and the method can be trained on simulated geometries whose contact labels are free. (3) **Point2CAD's split:** learn or estimate only the segmentation, then fit analytic models and intersect them to get topology, here the lower-dimensional strata. ComplexGen's chain complex of vertices, curves and patches with incidence is the 3D analogue of a stratification. The architectures are 3D-specific, but the formulations are not.

### Cited Findings
- **SPFN (CVPR 2019):** Predicts per-point properties (membership, type, normals), then a *differentiable model estimator* computes primitive types and parameters. It detects a varying number of primitives at different scales without manual tuning and is trained with ground-truth primitive membership. Primitive types are plane, sphere, cylinder and cone; data is ANSI mechanical parts. Code is at `lingxiaoli94/SPFN`. — [Li, Sung, Dubrovina, Yi, Guibas, arXiv 1811.08988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.08988)
- **ParSeNet (ECCV 2020):** Trained end to end to decompose point clouds into parametric patches, including B-spline patches as well as basic primitives, on a large dataset of man-made shapes. Code is at hippogriff.github.io/parsenet. — [Sharma et al., ECCV 2020](https://ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/html/186_ECCV_2020_paper.php); [arXiv 2003.12181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12181v1)
- **HPNet (ICCV 2021):** Combines a learned semantic descriptor, two **spectral descriptors** derived from predicted geometric parameters, and an **adjacency matrix encoding sharp edges**, with entropy-based learned weights. Final segmentation is by **mean-shift**, so no k. Evaluated on ANSI and ABCParts. — [Yan et al., ICCV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Yan_HPNet_Deep_Primitive_Segmentation_Using_Hybrid_Representations_ICCV_2021_paper.html)
- **SED-Net (SIGGRAPH 2023):** Detects surfaces, including B-spline patches, and edges jointly with two branches and two-stage fusion. Detected surfaces help locate edges and corners. Code, data and weights are released. — [SED-Net GitHub](https://github.com/yuanqili78/SED-Net); [SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=132405)
- **BPNet (IJCAI 2023):** Segments into Bézier primitives to avoid a fixed primitive catalogue, with joint segmentation and fitting and a soft-voting regularizer. — [Fu et al., arXiv 2307.04013](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.04013v1)
- **Point2CAD (CVPR 2024):** A pretrained segmentation backbone, then *learning-free* fitting of primitives and freeform surfaces (neural surface estimation). The fitted surfaces are extended and intersected analytically so that edges, corners and topology emerge. Results are state of the art on ABC. The authors note output quality depends heavily on segmentation, since segmentation outliers corrupt fitting and topology. Code is at YujiaLiu76/point2cad (about 425 stars, last push Sept 2024). — [Liu et al., arXiv 2312.04962](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.04962); [CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Liu_Point2CAD_Reverse_Engineering_CAD_Models_from_3D_Point_Clouds_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)
- **ComplexGen (SIGGRAPH 2022):** Treats a B-Rep as a *chain complex* of vertices, edges and faces with topology. A sparse-CNN encoder and tri-path transformer decoder predict all three element types and their incidences with probabilities, then a global optimization selects a valid complex and refines geometry. — [Guo et al., arXiv 2205.14573](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.14573); see also the survey [Geometric Deep Learning for CAD, arXiv 2402.17695](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17695)
- **NH-Rep (SIGGRAPH Asia 2022):** Represents a solid as a **Boolean tree over neural implicit functions**, one per B-Rep sub-patch; the zero level set of the root is the boundary. This preserves sharp edges and supports offsetting, blending and CSG. It was tested on about 10k CAD models. Patch-Grid notes it can fail on thin features. — [Guo et al., arXiv 2209.10191](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.10191); [Patch-Grid, arXiv 2308.13934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13934)
- **Neural-Singular-Hessian (SIGGRAPH Asia 2023):** For *unoriented* points, it forces the implicit function's Hessian to have zero determinant near the surface, as holds for a true SDF in a thin shell. The weight is annealed for fidelity, which suppresses ghost geometry. Code is at `bearprin/Neural-Singular-Hessian`. — [Wang et al., arXiv 2309.01793](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.01793); [GitHub](https://github.com/bearprin/Neural-Singular-Hessian)
- **SharpNet (arXiv Jan 2026; ACM DOI listed):** MLPs are globally smooth. SharpNet adds an auxiliary feature function, the solution of a Poisson equation with *jump Neumann* conditions, which is differentiable with respect to feature locations. Crease locations and MLP weights are optimized jointly, giving C0 creases only where specified. Evaluated on 2D problems and 3D CAD. — [Niu et al., arXiv 2601.19683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19683)

### Inferences
- **Min-of-K implicit networks (strongest transferable idea).** Per `peg_hole_analytic.py`, separation(q) = min over the four circle searches, and the contact set is {q : separation(q) = 0}. Mode membership is therefore the *argmin index*: a union-type CSG of per-feature-pair constraint functions, which is NH-Rep's representation. Proposal:
  1. Query the simulator for signed separation at arbitrary q, inside and outside contact, near the samples. The analytic module already computes this, and MuJoCo's distance query could too.
  2. Fit f_θ(q) = min_{k=1..K} f_k(q), or a soft-min annealed towards a hard min, with K overcomplete (say 16). Each f_k is a small MLP on the 4D reduced coordinates, with Eikonal or singular-Hessian regularization.
  3. Prune unused pieces with a label-cost-like sparsity penalty. Mode = argmin_k f_k(q_i) at each contact sample; two-contact strata are where the two smallest f_k agree within ε.
  This needs **no mode labels**. Creases fall where f_k swap, so crease and tangent-continuous boundaries are treated alike, and thin strata are not swallowed because they come from intersections of pieces rather than from clusters. Measured wrench supervises ∇f_k: the frictionless force is parallel to ∇ of the active piece. Caveat: the min-of-K decomposition is not unique; two pieces can split one true constraint sheet. The label-cost pruning plus a merge test on f_k agreement handles this, as in PEARL.
- **SPFN / HPNet-style sim-supervised segmentation.** Generate many random peg/hole geometries (radii, clearances, chamfers, depths) and run the existing sampler on each. Contact feature-pair labels then come free from the simulator. Train a point network on 4D (or 6D) coordinates plus wrench and normal features, with a discriminative embedding loss and a differentiable fit of the analytic constraint family (Q3). At test time cluster embeddings by mean-shift, which is k-free. PointNet++ and DGCNN-style operators take arbitrary input dimension (my reading of the architectures, not verified this session). This is "self-supervision from the simulator" in the sense of training on other geometries; it does not use the target's privileged labels.
- **Point2CAD lesson.** Use learning or graph partitioning only for single-contact sheets. Then fit analytic constraint models (Q3) and *compute* the two-contact strata as intersections of the fitted models, instead of clustering them. That removes the hardest thin clusters from the clustering problem.
- **ComplexGen mapping.** Chain-complex elements map to strata: 3D single-contact sheets correspond to faces, 2D two-contact strata to curves, and 1D or point three-contact strata to corners, with incidence between them. Predicting the stratum graph jointly, as the repo's "stratum graph" families already assume, is the analogue. A validity-constrained global selection (every intersection stratum lies on exactly two sheets) could clean noisy segmentations.
- **SharpNet** is an alternative to min-of-K for representing a single continuous separation or "contact-height" function over the configuration space with learned crease locations. Its feature curves come from optimization rather than from a CSG argmin.

### Gaps
- No found paper applies NH-Rep, SPFN or similar methods to configuration spaces or to dimensions above 3. The transfer is my proposal.
- How Neural-Singular-Hessian's regularizer generalizes to codimension above 1 (2D strata in 4D) is unclear; it assumes a hypersurface.
- I did not verify whether min-of-K training reliably finds the true number of constraint pieces without a strong sparsity prior. Results in the CvxNet, BSP-Net and CAPRI-Net line were not fetched this session.

## Q5. Contrastive or self-supervised embeddings on graphs where trajectory neighbours are positives (GraphSAGE-style, node2vec/DeepWalk on transition graphs, Markov-state methods), followed by clustering

### Takeaway
For **hook-in-eyelet** (modes separated by narrow passages), the most suitable tools come from Markov-state modelling of trajectories: transition-graph eigenfunctions, PCCA+ fuzzy metastable memberships, VAMPnets, and inductive Laplacian-representation learning (ALLO). Flow-based community detection (Infomap, Markov stability) fits too. Bottlenecks in flow are exactly what these detect. For **peg-in-hole**, trajectories cross mode boundaries freely because pose is continuous, so "trajectory neighbour = positive" teaches continuity, not mode identity. Positives must be gated by wrench continuity, and node features (wrench, normal) must enter the encoder, or the embedding inherits the pose-only failure (ARI about 0.3).

### Cited Findings
- **VAMPnets:** Use the variational approach for Markov processes with neural networks to map coordinates directly to (soft) Markov states end to end. This replaces featurization, reduction, clustering and MSM estimation, and gives few-state, interpretable kinetic models that match or beat standard MSM pipelines. GraphVAMPNet is a GNN follow-up. — [Mardt et al., arXiv 1710.06012](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.06012)
- **deeptime:** A scikit-learn-like Python library for MSMs, HMMs, Koopman/VAMP, VAMPnets and deep MSMs, with analysis of metastability, relaxation times and transition paths. — [Hoffmann et al., arXiv 2110.15013](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2110.15013)
- **PCCA+:** Produces a fuzzy membership matrix (non-negative rows summing to one) from the dominant eigenvectors of the transfer operator, whose eigenvalues near 1 form the Perron cluster. Crisp labels come from the largest membership. The number of macrostates is usually read from a gap in that eigenvalue spectrum. "It is not always possible to uniquely determine the boundaries of metastable regions." — [FU Berlin Markov seminar slides](https://ftp.mi.fu-berlin.de/pub/ag-cmb/markov-seminar-2021/slides/workshop2021-PCCA-TPT.pdf); [ZIB lecture notes](https://www.zib.de/userpage/donati/stochastics2023/12/lecture_notes/L12.pdf)
- **Proper Laplacian representation (ALLO, ICLR 2024):** Learns the smallest Laplacian eigenvectors *and eigenvalues* of a transition graph with a neural network. Earlier graph-drawing objectives depend on hard-to-tune hyperparameters and converge to arbitrary rotations of the eigenvectors; ALLO, an augmented-Lagrangian max-min objective, removes this. — [Gomez, Bowling, Machado, arXiv 2310.10833](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10833)
- **Infomap:** Optimizes the map equation on flow, including **state networks** for higher-order (memory) dynamics. — [Infomap docs](https://mapequation.org/infomap-python-docs/)
- **Markov stability / PyGenStability:** Markov time is the resolution parameter, and robust partitions persist across time scales. — [Delvenne et al., PNAS 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919907); [PyGenStability](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05385)
- **Known references, not re-fetched this session:** DeepWalk, truncated random-walk skip-gram node embeddings ([arXiv 1403.6652](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)). node2vec, with biased BFS/DFS walks ([arXiv 1607.00653](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00653)). GraphSAGE, inductive neighbourhood aggregation of node features with an unsupervised loss where random-walk co-occurrence gives positives ([arXiv 1706.02216](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)). Deep Graph Infomax ([arXiv 1809.10341](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10341)). DMoN, GNN clustering by modularity with a collapse regularizer ([arXiv 2006.16904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16904)). MinCutPool, a relaxed normalized-cut pooling ([arXiv 1907.00481](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00481)).

### Inferences
- **Hook-in-eyelet.** Build a transition graph from sampler walks: nodes are samples or fine kNN microstates, and edges are observed walk steps. Then (a) run Infomap or a PyGenStability time sweep for k-free metastable regions, or (b) take the leading eigenvectors (eigenvalues near 1) and run PCCA+, choosing the number of states from the gap and checking membership crispness. Fuzzy PCCA+ memberships also flag the narrow-passage samples (memberships near 0.5), which may be useful. For 1e6 samples, cluster to about 1e4 microstates first (k-means in pose), as in standard MSM practice, or learn the eigenfunctions inductively with ALLO or VAMPnets.
- **Peg-in-hole: gate positives by physics.** Define positives as sample pairs that are adjacent on a walk *and* have no wrench jump between them (Q2 edge statistic below threshold). Define hard negatives as pose-near samples (inside the kNN set) *with* a wrench jump. A GraphSAGE or DGI encoder over the kNN+trajectory graph, with node features (pose, wrench direction, sign-free normal, surface variation, curvature), trained with an InfoNCE loss on these pairs, learns an embedding where modes are compact. Cluster with HDBSCAN or Leiden-CPM on the embedding kNN graph, both k-free. This is a learned version of the Q1 signed-cost recipe. The same labels could feed a multicut directly, which is cheaper and more transparent, so the contrastive route pays off mainly if an *inductive* model is needed (new samples, new geometries).
- **Avoid plain DeepWalk/node2vec on the pose kNN graph.** Their embeddings are smoothed proximity in the same graph that gave ARI about 0.3, so they cannot add information. Walks would need edges reweighted by wrench continuity, which brings back Q2's edge statistic.
- **Avoid DMoN and MinCutPool as the final clusterer.** They need a maximum cluster count and inherit modularity or normalized-cut balance biases, the same failure modes already seen.

### Gaps
